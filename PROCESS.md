# Process — 御风 / Windborne

## Starting idea

This individual project began with the particle bamboo-forest visual language of my earlier martial-arts carnival project. I reduced the scope to one playable encounter: a straw-hatted rider standing on a sword, controlled by small head movements, dodging twelve attacks.

The intended user is someone seated at a laptop. The intended effect is to make a physical gesture visibly change the rider's position and produce understandable feedback. Keyboard mode provides the same loop when the camera is unavailable.

## Decisions and revisions

The Git commits record actual development stages; the history has not been recreated to imply earlier work.

1. **Playable loop first.** Instead of continuing static storyboards, implement four practice gestures, a 60-second trial, scoring, pause/resume and restart. The author chose discrete dodges rather than continuous steering.
2. **Readable action.** The initial scene used pale scenery and linear attack markers. Author feedback described it as monotonous and the timing as unclear. It became a black space with flowing green/gold particles, a particle rider and a giant opponent that visibly winds up and swings.
3. **Camera reliability.** Fix a strict-mode diagnostic callback in the vendored WASM loader. Add camera preview, face bounding box, device selection and restart. Separate actual negative detections from slow inference. Add automatic local compatibility inference when a Worker fails or times out.
4. **Correct direction.** Author testing found right turns moved left and looking up moved down. Reverse the shared yaw/pitch signs. Add tests from camera matrix through calibration offset and gesture gating to rider displacement.
5. **Small downward gestures.** Author feedback found downward movement hard to trigger. Lower its threshold from 9° to 6°, shorten its hold requirement and expose live direction-strength indicators.
6. **Timing and latency.** Feedback reported that users struggled to dodge. Move the actionable warning to one second before impact, display remaining time and extend the trial dodge to 1.4 seconds so early action does not return to centre before impact. Request lower-resolution video, process fresh frames without backlog, raise sampling limits and reduce smoothing delay. Simulated-delay tests do not prove measured real-camera latency.
7. **Keepsake revision.** The original export contained the game scene; its image and download were confirmed by the author. The latest requested version captures the user's camera frame and adds a score-based joke, such as “剑气的已读不回”. It evaluates game performance, not appearance. Images are kept locally, and a missing portrait produces an honest no-photo card.

The earlier promise that camera images were excluded from exports applied only to the original game-scene keepsake; the current home screen and README describe portrait capture explicitly.

## AI tools and choices

I used **OpenAI Codex** for implementation assistance, concept discussion, Three.js/React code, MediaPipe integration, tests, debugging and documentation. I supplied the concept, selected interaction choices, tested the experience and requested revisions.

- **Kept:** build a working interaction before polishing visuals; use one shared game loop for camera and keyboard; provide local fallback and explicit feedback.
- **Changed/rejected:** continuous movement became a discrete dodge; storyboard-first development was abandoned; dense realistic particles became procedural flowing points; a short-video keepsake was rejected in favour of PNG; the original game-scene keepsake later became a user-requested face card.
- No AI-generated raster assets, movie clips or paid scene-generation services are included. Particle shapes are generated by code. The MediaPipe model is a third-party ML dependency, credited in CREDITS.md.

AI-produced code was revised after concrete failures. Tests are supporting evidence, not a substitute for user trials or actual camera testing.

## Testing evidence

At the initial upload, 38 automated tests pass and the TypeScript/Vite production build succeeds. Coverage includes gesture rearming, four directions, attack boundaries, pause, scoring, simulated timing delay, slow/lost tracking, Worker fallback, cleanup, portrait capture and caption tiers.

Author feedback confirmed keyboard practice and an earlier game-scene PNG export. Camera failures, reversed controls, poor downward response and high latency were reported and led to the changes above. The included screenshot is an earlier recovery/pause screen supplied by the author, with no face image. It is not evidence that the newest visuals or portrait export have been approved.

## Required other-person trial — not yet fully documented

The brief requires at least one other person to try the experience **before it is explained**. That requirement cannot be marked complete from the current record. A message reported that “users found it hard to dodge”, but it did not establish the trial setup, who tried first without explanation, or the observations needed below. I will not invent those details.

Before final submission, record a real trial without identifying or recording the participant:

- Date and device/browser: **to record**.
- What they did before receiving instructions: **to observe**.
- Where they hesitated and what they thought the feedback meant: **to observe**.
- Whether they understood the one-second warning, neutral return, pause and download: **to observe**.
- One specific observation: **to record**.
- The resulting code/design change, or a reasoned decision to keep it: **to complete**.

Suggested trial: let them start, complete practice, play one minute and save a card. Then test denied camera permission or moving out of frame. Keep portraits and recordings out of the public repository.
